Use case: infographic-diagram
Asset type: one LinkedIn feed image, portrait 4:5, paired with a French post about a full CRM that does not guide the sales team's next action.
Primary request: Create a premium editorial business visual that makes the gap between stored prospect data and a clear commercial action instantly legible. This is a conceptual diagram, not a software screenshot.
Canvas and layout: Portrait 4:5. Reserve the top 25% for two bold, perfectly legible lines of typography. In the middle, show a dense orderly wall of small anonymous prospect cards in muted slate. One card is selected; a thin warm-gold route leaves it, passes through three simple decision checkpoints, and ends as a clear forward arrow near the lower edge. Generous outer margins and a calm empty footer. Do not place any other text on the cards or checkpoints.
Style and medium: Sophisticated architectural editorial illustration with subtle 3D depth, precise geometry and tactile paper/metal surfaces. High contrast, restrained detail, credible for a B2B founder's LinkedIn profile. The deep navy and warm-gold contrast echoes existing published project imagery, without copying its split-room composition.
Color palette: deep navy, charcoal and slate for the background and data cards; ivory for the headline; one restrained warm-gold accent for the decision route.
Text (verbatim, only visible words): first line "CRM PLEIN." second line "PROCHAINE ACTION ?". Large modern sans serif, crisp French spelling, no other letters, numbers or logos anywhere.
Constraints: The selected card and route must be the focal point after the headline. The gold line must connect the selected card to the arrow in a visually coherent direction. Keep the core content within safe margins for LinkedIn cropping.
Avoid: stock-photo people, robots, glowing brains, fake dashboards, real CRM brands, invented metrics, tiny labels, decorative icons, visual clutter, watermarks, garbled typography.

Reference Gallery inspiration for the fixed-zone diagram structure: No. 69, "Museum catalog disassembly infographic", Author: @MrLarus, Source: https://x.com/MrLarus. The subject, palette and composition above are adapted for this project; do not copy the reference image.
